01

The grain

One stalk of grain, leaned thirty-two degrees, the way a pointer leans. It is the pointer on grain.school, the favicon and the seal at the foot of a letter. This draft puts four candidates side by side, all drawn from the same kernel. Pick one here, from the dock, or with [ and ], and the whole book redraws with it.

At every size

From 16 cells up, each cut is the one path sampled on that grid. The 8 and 12 cuts are set by hand, because sampling loses the shape that small. Below 24 the crease is left out.

Room

Leave half a grain clear on every side. The smallest the vector mark goes is 16 px; under that, use the 12-cell pixel cut.

02

Letters

Three faces, all monospaced or cut on a grid, and each with serifs. One is for the name, one is for reading and one is for the machine. All three are under the SIL Open Font License, so they can be shipped, embedded and changed.

lesson 04 · the long season

Sowing in rows

A row is a promise to come back. You sow it in a straight line so you can find it again, and so the weeds show up as weeds. The same goes for notes, code and days.

03

Dyes

Two grounds: Linen by day and Hearth by night. On them go five plant dyes, used one at a time. There is no blue: the palette stays on the warm side of the field. Click any swatch to copy it.

04

Elements

Few parts, drawn plainly. Corners are softly rounded. A rule is a row of grains, and a frame is one fine line with its name set into the top. Anything the machine says is set in the console face.

05

Motion

Most of the time nothing moves. When a whole view changes it settles once, over one second. When you touch something it responds where you touched it, and briefly. If you have asked your device for less motion, every one of these stays still.

06

In the world

The grain on paper, on walls and in feeds. The card and the poster are drawn as files and print at size.

07

Interfaces

Three sketches of a computer that grows things instead of opening windows. They show the brand at full scale, and they ask what an operating system would be like if it were laid out like a field rather than a desk.
